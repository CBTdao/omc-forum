The largest IPO ever is a down payment: Anthropic's $81.3B debut and $900B of compute commitments (2026)

On November 19, 2026, Anthropic became the first frontier AI lab to list publicly — with the largest IPO in market history. The record number everyone quotes is the raise. The number that actually organizes the story sits in the filing: nearly $900 billion of compute commitments, against which the biggest IPO ever raised covers roughly an eighth.

DCF EditorialNov 22, 202612 min read
TL;DR

The record is the raise; the story is the spread between the raise and the compute bill. Anthropic priced on 2026-11-18 at $83 (above the $70–78 range), sold 980 million shares, and raised $81.3 billion — the largest IPO ever, beating SpaceX's June listing ($75B, rising to $86B with the greenshoe). Add a $26 billion private placement (AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia, TSMC) and the total deal is $107.3 billion. Day one on Nasdaq (ticker ANT): opened $111.50 (+34%), closed $97.17 (+17.07%) while the broad market sold off. The filing behind the debut (verified via Reuters, The Star's Reuters wire and Le Monde): annualized revenue run-rate of $81B as of October 31 — up $10B in a single month; Q3 revenue $16.96B (+47% QoQ); operating income swung from +$974M to −$567M in one quarter; first-half compute spend of ~$9B (roughly 4× a year earlier) plus ~$5B more in Q3; 11,900 customers at ≥$100K TTM (vs ~1,500 a year ago); and nearly $900B in compute commitments with Amazon and Google. Our arithmetic: $107.3B ÷ $900B ≈ 12% — the largest IPO in history funds about an eighth of the announced compute bill, which is why the private placement exists and why management is already talking about debt. At the $83 price, the ~$1.5T fully diluted valuation is about 18.5× the annualized run-rate (ours).

What happened, in verified numbers. Anthropic priced its IPO on Wednesday, November 18, 2026 at $83 per share, above an initial range of $70–78, after increasing the offering about 19% to 980 million shares — roughly 956 million new shares from the company and the balance from selling shareholders, netting the company about $79.4 billion, per Reuters and Le Monde. That makes the $81.3 billion IPO the largest in history, surpassing Elon Musk's SpaceX, which raised $75 billion in June at a $1.77 trillion valuation (its total reached $86 billion after its underwriters' option was exercised). Anthropic's total deal size is $107.3 billion, including a $26 billion private placement whose investors are the supply chain itself: AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC. Underwriters led by Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley hold an option on up to 147 million more shares, which would lift the IPO alone to about $93.5 billion. All figures cross-checked across Reuters, The Star's Reuters wire, Le Monde, CGTN and Xinhua-wire reports, which agree on every headline number.

What did day one actually price?

Trading opened Thursday, November 19 at $111.50 — 34% above the IPO price — putting the fully diluted valuation near $2 trillion, and closed at $97.17, up 17.07%, on a day the Dow fell 2.19% and the Nasdaq 2.25%. Reuters notes that on an outstanding-shares basis the company briefly crossed $1.5 trillion, "on par with Tesla"; at the $83 offer price the fully diluted valuation was about $1.5 trillion and about $1.1 trillion non-diluted, per the Reuters wire. Two pieces of context sharpen the read. First, demand was genuine: Bloomberg's tally puts subscription demand near seven times the offering, and grey-market trading had priced shares about 20% above the offer before the open. Second, the pop was bought against a market actively selling AI risk — Nvidia has been reeling since CEO Jensen Huang's resignation, and investors openly question whether infrastructure spending converts to profit. A 17% close, not a 34% close, is what that tension looks like when it settles. Our arithmetic on the closing valuation: fully diluted shares implied by Reuters' "$2 trillion at the open" figure work out to roughly 18 billion shares, which puts the $97.17 close near $1.75 trillion fully diluted — consistent with the Chinese wire reports that ranked the company twelfth globally by that measure.

What does the filing say about the compute bill?

The customer-side number deserves its own paragraph because it is the demand-side validation under the whole valuation: 11,900 customers generated at least $100,000 in trailing-twelve-month revenue as of September 30, against just over 1,500 at the end of last year — roughly an eightfold expansion in twelve months (ours), and a base now broad enough that no single whale's renewal can swing the quarter. The Reuters wire attributes the growth to surging demand for the Claude models, "particularly Claude Code," the coding agent that has become the developer-market entry point. Broad-based six-figure customers are also the practical answer to the circular-deal critique: whatever the vendor ecosystem wires back into the private placement, recurring revenue at this customer count has to come from organizations running real workloads.

This is the part this forum cares about, and it is unusually specific for an IPO filing. Revenue: the annualized run-rate reached $81 billion as of October 31 — up $10 billion from September 30, a month-over-month addition larger than many AI companies' entire revenue. Q3 revenue was $16.96 billion, up 47% from Q2; the first nine months totaled $33.2 billion, twelve times the year-ago period. Costs: the company spent nearly $9 billion on computing capacity and infrastructure in the first half of 2026 — almost four times a year earlier — and incurred an additional ~$5 billion of compute costs in Q3, which per the Reuters wire is why the revenue generated per dollar of compute capacity declined. Commitments: nearly $900 billion in computing commitments with partners including Amazon and Google. And the operating line tells the quarter's real story: Q2 booked +$974M of operating income; Q3 swung to −$567M of operating loss — a $1.5 billion reversal in a single quarter, during the same quarter that growth decelerated sharply. The widely quoted "net loss narrowed to $23.3 billion from $66.3 billion" is dominated by non-operating items on convertible bonds; the operating line is the cleaner signal, and it points the other way.

Why the biggest IPO ever still looks like a down payment

Put the two verified numbers side by side (our arithmetic): the total raise of $107.3 billion against nearly $900 billion of compute commitments is about 12%. Even the maximum greenshoe outcome (~$93.5 billion IPO-only) stays in the same band. That single ratio explains three otherwise odd facts. It explains the private placement: the seven supply-chain investors — AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia, TSMC — wired $26 billion not primarily as a financial bet but as committed customers pre-funding the capacity they intend to sell into. It explains the debt talk: CEO Dario Amodei told CNBC on listing day, "At some point in the future we're hoping to be investment grade, and debt is an instrument that we have available to us, and we'd like to use at some point in the future." And it explains the structure of this forum's supply-chain thread: the money Anthropic has committed sits, on the sell side, in NVIDIA's own disclosures — the $390 billion of purchase obligations and $118 billion of guarantees we covered last week are the upstream mirror of exactly these downstream commitments. The LTA chain the DRAM post documented now has its cleanest end-to-end example: customer prepayments and IPO proceeds become cloud commitments, cloud commitments become chip and memory purchase obligations, purchase obligations become supply commitments and guarantees — every link numbered, every link larger than the last.

How to read the 18.5×

At the $83 offer price, the ~$1.5 trillion fully diluted valuation is about 18.5× the $81 billion annualized run-rate (ours); annualized off Q3's $16.96 billion instead ($67.8 billion) and it is roughly 22× (ours). The multiple is demanding but not unprecedented for a company whose run-rate added $10 billion in a month; the question the market is actually pricing is the shape of the next twelve months. Three variables decide it, all visible in public disclosures. One: whether the run-rate keeps compounding at anything near its recent $10B/month pace — October's number is one data point, not a trend. Two: whether the compute-per-revenue-dollar ratio stabilizes once Anthropic's own committed capacity (rather than spot purchases) comes online — the filing's own framing is that Q3's $5B of incremental compute costs diluted revenue per capacity dollar. Three: whether the $900B commitment stack gets renegotiated as cheaper hardware arrives, the way every previous infrastructure buyer has tried to do. Day one answered none of these; it only established that the market will fund the first installment. The CEO's own framing at the bell — "Today puts the company in a stronger financial position" — is accurate and deliberately modest: a stronger position, not a solved one. Summary: Anthropic raised the largest amount any IPO has ever raised, $107.3 billion including its private placement, and it covers roughly 12% of the compute commitments behind its own filing — the frontier AI business model now has a market-set price on its down payment, and 2026's last great question is who funds the rest.

  • Day one: open $111.50 (+34%), close $97.17 (+17.07%) — against a market that fell 2%+ the same day; fully diluted ~$2T at the open, ~$1.5T at the offer price, ~$1.75T at the close (ours from Reuters inputs).
  • The filing: run-rate $81B (Oct 31, +$10B in a month); Q3 revenue $16.96B (+47% QoQ); 11,900 customers ≥$100K TTM vs ~1,500 a year ago — but operating income swung from +$974M to −$567M, with ~$9B first-half compute spend (4× YoY) plus ~$5B in Q3.
  • Nearly $900B of compute commitments vs a $107.3B raise ≈ 12% coverage (ours) — the record raise is a down payment, which is why the supply chain itself wired the private placement and why management is already discussing debt.
